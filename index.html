The urban problem

🏙️ Rapid urbanization

Built-up land expands while vegetation shrinks.

🔥 Urban heat islands

Hard surfaces keep cities warmer than surroundings.

🏭 Air pollution

Traffic and industry raise exposure in dense areas.

🚶 Less outdoor activity

Few shaded, walkable spaces reduce time outside.

🤝 Social isolation

Fewer shared public spaces weaken neighbourhood ties.

⚖️ Unequal access

Some neighbourhoods are far from any green space.

Public health

Physical health

Shaded, green routes can support walking, cycling and outdoor recreation, and may reduce heat exposure.

Mental health

Nature exposure is associated with stress reduction and psychological restoration.

Environmental health

Vegetation may contribute to better air quality, cooler temperatures and lower noise.

Jaipur context

Public context gathered for this prototype. The app is NOT connected to Nagar Nigam Jaipur. Please re-verify each point on official sources before citing it.

Administrative structure

A 2019 state notification split Nagar Nigam Jaipur into Heritage (100 wards) and Greater (150 wards), 250 wards in total. Check the current structure on the official website before citing it.

Green-cover target

An older news report states that Jaipur Master Plan 2025 expects 20% green cover while the city had about 11.3%. Use it as context only; confirm with JDA / official figures.

Ward-level data path

A 150-ward boundary file (KML) for Greater Jaipur is listed on the OpenCity open-data portal. Joining such boundaries with air-quality and tree data is a realistic future integration.

Research insights

Methodology

Data collection→Cleaning→Exploratory analysis→Feature selection→Scoring / recommendation→GIS mapping→Visualization→Interpretation

Research questions

  1. How can urban environmental data help prioritize green infrastructure?
  2. How can green spaces contribute to public health?
  3. How can they support social cohesion?
  4. Which factors should influence tree recommendation?
  5. How can GIS visualization support planning?

Green Priority Score

Pollution 25% + Temperature 20% + Low green coverage 20% + Population density 15% + Open space 10% + Traffic/pedestrian 10%. Each factor is normalized to 0–100. High ≥ 60, Medium 45–59, Low < 45. This is a Rule-Based Green Priority Model, not machine learning.

Data sources

Now: simulated sample data (seeded generator in js/data.js), OpenStreetMap tiles.
Possible later: municipal GIS, tree census, air-quality stations, weather data, satellite imagery. Designed for future municipal GIS/API integration.

Future municipal integration

Municipal GIS/API→Ingestion→Cleaning→Feature engineering→Green Priority Model→Tree recommendation→GIS visualization→Decision dashboard

Future ML: Random Forest, XGBoost, regression or clustering for green priority, heat vulnerability and tree suitability; later satellite imagery and remote sensing.

Limitations & about

  • Tree choice needs expert validation
  • Real use needs validated data and permissions
